## G. Implications

_(written by hand after the run)_

1. **Output sets, not a best match; recall matters.** Only 5.6% of S1 are singletons (identical in US/India), mean 3.46 matches, 71% of entities have 2–5, p90 = 6 (B1/B2). The decoder must pick k per entity; a top-1 policy caps recall at ~1/3.5. The singleton rate is the prior for the empty gate; stratify dev/OOF samples by k-bucket (0 / 1–2 / 3–4 / 5+).
2. **One-to-one is exact** (0 of 7.64M matched pool ids shared, B3) → keep the global one-to-one step and the "competition" stage-2 feature. **~26% of pool records match nothing** (B4, the same for S2/S3 and both countries) → the model needs negatives from unmatched pool records, not only other entities' matches.
3. **Names alone are not enough.** 48–54% of S1 share their exact (country, name_core) with another S1 (D1). 60.5% of sampled S1 have ≥1 exact-name pool record that is *not* a match, mean 56, p99 757 (D3). Only 57% of true pairs have equal name_core (C). Address agreement features (street_key_eq on 60% of pairs, num_eq on 69%, addr Jaccard p50 0.89) and the name-frequency feature are mandatory. Exact name equality must not dominate: hard-negative mining should oversample same-name, different-address candidates.
4. **The noise channel is real and measurable** (C, % of pairs): digit deletion 5.5%, house number missing on one side 9.9%, legal missing on one side 21.8%, legal moved 3.2%, one-edit typo 4.7%, token reorder 3.4%, generic-only diff 3.6%, **no shared core token 8.0%** (trade-name swap). Keep all the `f_nz_*` features. The 8% name-disjoint pairs can only be recovered by address keys/features, so `addr_only` must stay.
5. **Country-specific behaviour the model has to learn without a country feature:** India has legal_differs 13.1% vs US 0.6% (pvt/private/ltd variants → canonicalise more legal synonyms). India addr_norm_eq is 8.7% vs US 30.1%, num_other 18.9% vs 8.6%, landmark 9.3% vs 0%, pool name non-Latin 17.9%. Use the shape of the address (token count, landmark flag, script flag) as features instead of country, so France (addr p50 = 8 tokens, between US 6 and India 10) sits inside the training range.
6. **Postcode is dead in this data**: postcode_found is ≤ 1.9% in every split/country (A2) and 99.8% of pairs have a postcode missing (C). France also has 0.3%. Drop the postcode feature group and the `p` blocking family (0.16% pair coverage, F2), I checked the extractor: raw France S1 addresses have no postcodes either (89 of 20,000 contain any 5-digit run; the format is "175 Boulevard du Président Franklin Roosevelt, Bordeaux, Nouvelle-Aquitaine"). French house numbers can carry `bis`/`ter` suffixes ("5 bis Rue …"), so `street_key` should skip those tokens.
7. **The generic-vocab fit surfaced an alias pattern**: `aka, dba, fka, formerly, known, nee, doing` are in the learned generic set (C header). Names contain "X dba Y" / "formerly known as" aliases. Add a normalization rule that splits aliases into a second name and scores max(sim(name, alias)). Also drop 1-char tokens (`a, b, f, k, m`) and transliteration junk (`lkssmii`, `jy`) from the generic vocab (min token length 3).
8. **Blocking: the df cut is not what loses recall; the top-40 truncation is.** 98.4% of true pairs share ≥1 key that survives df ≤ 2000 (F2). E01 block recall was 0.90, so ~8 pt is lost to ranking/top-k, not to key coverage. Next: raise the weights of the families with unique recall, `a` (2.5% of pairs reachable only through it) and `ab` (1.7%, df p99 = 60, very selective). Consider a separate address-channel top-k. The `n`/`np`/`a` families lose 64%/75%/77% of postings to the df cut, and `nm` is only usable on 21% of pairs; its weight could be lowered.
9. **France is the main risk: vocabulary shift is larger than our cross-country proxy.** France name_core token OOV vs all of train is 45.3% (74% of records have ≥1 OOV core token), against 27.6% for US→India-vocab and 36.3% for India→US-vocab. Test US/India OOV is only 2.6–3.0%. Address OOV (23.1%) matches the US↔India proxy (22.6%). Features built on learned train vocabularies (generic vocab, translit, token IDF from train) will silently not fire for France. Compute IDF / name frequency on the *test* pool per country label, and prefer character-level similarities (Jaro-Winkler, char n-grams) over token identity.
10. **French dictionary gaps** (E2, top OOV name tokens in France): `ecole, amicale, sportive, groupe, developpement, primaire, pharmacie, etablissements, lycee`. Add `etablissements/ets` as a legal-like prefix and consider French generic words (`groupe, ecole, association, amicale`) for the generic vocab; the learned vocab cannot contain them. 36.6% of France records have no recognised legal suffix. `sarl 23%, sas 16%, eurl 6%, sa 5%, sasu 4%, sci 4%` are recognised, so the legal dictionary coverage looks fine.
11. **US/India test ≈ train** (A2/E: script share, empty address 1.6–3.0%, length quantiles identical within ±1 token, OOV ≤ 3%). OOF estimates for US/India should transfer. The public-LB gap will be driven by France (15% of test S1: 259k entities).
